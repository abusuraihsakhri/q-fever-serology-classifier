Key diagnostic principles

A ≥4-fold phase II IgG rise on paired IFA supports acute Q fever. A single phase II IgG ≥1:128 is supportive, not definitive.

Phase I IgG ≥1:1024 requires assessment for a persistent focus. Titers alone do not establish chronic Q fever.

Negative early serology does not exclude infection. IgM and relative phase dominance alone are insufficient for diagnosis.

Source: CDC: Clinical and Laboratory Diagnosis for Q Fever (May 2024). Laboratory-specific thresholds and clinical consultation take precedence.
